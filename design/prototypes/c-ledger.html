Where it fits

For the titles that never made it to a digital edition.

Pergament was built around the ordinary case: a book exists on paper, the file does not, and commissioning a scanning bureau costs more than the title will earn back.

01

Out-of-print backlist

Titles you still hold the rights to but no longer stock. Scan the archive copy, list the EPUB, and the book is available again without a reprint.

02

Regional and small-run imprints

Print runs too small to justify a digitisation contract. Per-page pricing means a 90-page local history costs eighteen euros to convert.

03

A single archive copy

One fragile copy that cannot leave the building. Nothing is shipped, nothing is disbound — a phone above the page is the whole apparatus.

What to expect

An honest account of what the pipeline does well.

Optical recognition is very good at some pages and unreliable on others. You should know which is which before you scan a whole title.

Comes through cleanly

  • Continuous body text in Slovene or English
  • Standard serif and sans text faces at reading size
  • Chapter openings and simple paragraph structure
  • Covers, once you have squared the corners yourself

Needs your eye afterwards

  • Tables, indexes and multi-column setting
  • Footnotes, marginalia and running apparatus
  • Display type, blackletter and heavy letterspacing
  • Rights clearance — that part stays with you

Questions

What publishers ask before the first title.

Why does capture happen on a phone?

The camera has to be held above a page, and the guide has to warn you in the moment when a frame is too dark, too bright, blurred or shadowed. A phone does both; a desktop does neither. Open the site on a computer and you get a QR code that hands the session to your phone — payment, receipts and delivery work from either device.

Do I have to remove page numbers and running heads?

Frame the text block inside the guide and they fall outside the shot. That keeps folios and headers out of the recognised text without any extra work.

Can I interrupt a book and finish it later?

Yes. The session is held on the phone that started it, so you will be offered the choice to continue where you stopped or begin again.

What happens to the photographs?

They are processed into your EPUB and removed when the seven-day download window closes. Because there are no accounts, there is no archive of past scans to keep, audit or lose.

Who is responsible for the rights?

You are. Pergament is a conversion service, not a licence. Scan only what you hold the rights to, or what is in the public domain.
